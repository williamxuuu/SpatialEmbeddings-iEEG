# BaRISTA picks a scale; nobody tracks how it moves

The central proposal has not been done. No iEEG, EEG or MEG paper we found crosses the resolution of the spatial encoding with the amount of data (electrodes per region, number of subjects) and reports how the best resolution moves. The two closest papers stop short in specific, citable ways. **BaRISTA** compared channel-coordinate, Destrieux-parcel and lobe encodings on 10 Brain Treebank subjects and found parcels best, but it ran its data-scaling sweep only on the winning configuration and used no shuffled or matched-count controls ([BaRISTA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12135)). **MAPA** ablated a single DKT region embedding against within-shaft RoPE, with one seed and 7 pretraining subjects, and compared no other atlas or resolution ([MAPA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13507)). Proposals 1 (resolution × data density), 3 (probing learned ID embeddings), 5 (optimization vs representation) and soft region assignment all look novel for iEEG. Proposals 2 (matched-count controls) and 4 (task locality) are partly done: fMRI has random, geometric and rotated-null parcellation baselines and target-dependent optimal parcel counts, and one NeurIPS 2026 workshop paper already pits learned, signal-derived electrode coordinates against region labels and MNI coordinates ([Javadzadeh et al.](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157293)). The bias-variance prediction itself rests on textbook theory: for partition-based estimators the optimal cell side shrinks with sample size ([Györfi et al.](https://www.szit.bme.hu/~gyorfi/bookestpre.pdf)). The positioning follows from this. BaRISTA and MAPA answer "which spatial code works on Brain Treebank?" This project asks *why* a code works and *when* the answer changes, so it should present itself as a mechanism study that explains their results rather than a competing architecture.

## BaRISTA and MAPA answer "which scale," not "when"

**BaRISTA (NeurIPS 2025)** is the paper reviewers will compare this project against. It adds a learned embedding for each spatial category to every channel token and crosses three encoding scales with three masking scales in a 3×3 design ([BaRISTA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12135)). Parcel encoding won clearly: with channel masking, sentence-onset AUC was **0.862 for parcels, 0.842 for lobes and 0.778 for channels**, and speech/non-speech AUC was 0.869, 0.841 and 0.764. A two-way ANOVA found the encoding effect significant at p < 1e-3, and the encoding mattered more than the masking ([BaRISTA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12135)). Three details open room for this project. First, BaRISTA's "channel" condition is **not a per-electrode ID**. It is a discretized LPI coordinate in which each axis (integers 0 to 200) has its own lookup table and the three axis embeddings are summed. Its poor score therefore shows that fine per-axis coordinate lookup fails at this data size, not that per-electrode IDs fail. Second, the data density is low and reported but never analyzed: subjects have **19 to 47 Destrieux parcels holding 72 to 205 channels, roughly 3.5 to 4.4 electrodes per parcel**, and the paper does not relate the outcome to electrodes per region. Third, the 5 to 75% pretraining-data sweep covers only the best model (parcel encoding, channel masking), so it cannot show a scale × data interaction ([BaRISTA](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.12135)). BaRISTA also has no controls beyond random initialization, reports no seed variance, analyzes no convergence, tests only language/auditory tasks in the main text, and lists simpler sensory tasks as future work.

**MAPA (NeurIPS 2026)** uses a vanilla ViT-S MAE with a 75-row learned DKT region table (62 cortical regions, 12 subcortical structures and one out-of-vocabulary row) plus RoPE over contact index within each shaft. It pretrains on **27.9 h from 7 Brain Treebank subjects** ([MAPA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13507); [model card](https://huggingface.co/bentang18/MAPA)). Its 2×2 ablation, one run per model, shows the region embedding adding only about **+0.003 to +0.009 macro-AUROC on top of RoPE**. Against the no-spatial model it adds a larger **+0.018 to +0.026**. The key finding is that with neither encoding the pretrained model **falls below the non-pretrained frontend baseline in every regime** ([MAPA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13507)). The cross-subject gains are not significant after correction (q = 0.0625, 5 subjects). One confound matters for any comparison: MAPA's cross-subject readout averages contact features within DKT regions and fits on regions the two subjects share, so the atlas shapes the evaluation as well as the encoding. Only 50 of the 74 regions appear in pretraining. MAPA tries no other atlas, coordinates, per-channel IDs, shuffled labels, data sweep or soft assignment, and it does not analyze its learned embeddings.

The broader landscape splits into coordinate-based codes and category-based codes, with almost no head-to-head comparison between them. **PopT** encodes LPI coordinates with sinusoids, and removing them costs about 0.1 AUC across all four Brain Treebank tasks (for example, sentence onset drops from 0.90 to 0.81). Like BaRISTA, it ran its data sweep only on the full model ([PopT](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03044v4)). Seegnificant uses Gaussian-RBF MNI encodings over 21 subjects, and its position ablation reportedly cost little and was not significant. That detail is snippet-level and was not checked against Fig. 6A ([Seegnificant](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.10458)). DIVER-1 ran a scaling-law study over subject count without varying the spatial encoding ([DIVER-1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.19097v3)). The positioning sentence that follows is: *BaRISTA established that the scale of the spatial code matters, and MAPA that some spatial code is needed for MAE pretraining to beat baseline. We show how the optimal scale depends on data density and task locality, and separate the optimization benefit of spatial information from its representational benefit.*

### Proposal 1: the theory is textbook, the empirical curve is unclaimed

A lookup-table region embedding is a piecewise-constant function of location, which is exactly a regressogram. Partitioning-estimate theory therefore applies directly. Consistency requires cells to shrink (h → 0) while each still holds many samples (n·h^d → ∞). Balancing variance of order 1/(n·h^d) against squared bias of order h² gives an optimal cell side **h ∝ n^(-1/(d+2))** ([Györfi et al.](https://www.szit.bme.hu/~gyorfi/bookestpre.pdf); [bound as applied in arXiv 1810.13259](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.13259)). In 3-D brain space this predicts the optimal parcel count growing roughly as **K\* ∝ n^(3/5)**, sublinear in the number of training electrodes. The 1-D histogram results, with bin width shrinking as n^(-1/3), are the classic citations ([Scott's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott%27s_rule); [Wand 1997](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~rnugent/PCMI2016/papers/WandBinWidth.pdf)). Empirical-Bayes target encoding, which shrinks sparse categories toward the global mean by their counts, is the ML version of the same idea ([Pargent et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00180-022-01207-6)).

The empirical near-misses support the direction without demonstrating it. In fMRI, Dohmatob et al. found an optimal parcel cardinality of about 200 for rest-to-task prediction, an effect "pronounced in the small-sample regime" that "diminishes as the sample size increases". That statement is about the *size of the benefit* of the optimum, not about the optimum moving, and the full text was not read ([Dohmatob et al. 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348638654_Brain_topography_beyond_parcellations_Local_gradients_of_functional_maps)). Thirion et al. showed that likelihood criteria favor 3000 or more parcels while reproducibility peaks near 200. That is the cleanest citation for "fit favors fine, stability favors coarse", but the paper never varied the amount of data ([Thirion et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4076743/)). An ABCD structural-MRI study states explicitly that "the influence of sample size on the observed parcellation performance scaling remains unknown" ([Cerebral Cortex 2022](https://doi.org/10.1093/cercor/bhac060)). The closest spatial grain × N interaction found anywhere comes from ecology: species-distribution models trained on larger samples were better *and* more sensitive to coarsening grain ([Guisan et al. 2007](https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/sensitivity-of-predictive-species-distribution-models-to-change-i/)). Spike population models (NDT2, NDT3, POYO) use the finest identity granularity and report saturation with more sessions, but none compares granularities against data size ([arXiv 2407.14668](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.14668)).

Two caveats should shape the claim. First, an ML counterexample warns against assuming monotonicity on every capacity axis: in recommender models the optimal embedding *dimension* reportedly shrinks as data grows. That concerns embedding width, not partition granularity, and is snippet-level ([Ardalani et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.08489)). Second, iEEG breaks two assumptions of the classical theory. Electrodes cluster by subject, so the effective n is closer to the number of subjects than the number of electrodes. Test subjects also land in regions with no training coverage, which adds a term with no i.i.d. analogue and pushes the optimum coarser. The paper should state both as extensions to the theory rather than hide them.

### Proposal 2: random and cube partitions are serious competitors, not strawmen

fMRI has already run the matched-K contest, and anatomy did not win. Arslan et al. compared 34 parcellation methods on HCP. On homogeneity, **the Desikan and Destrieux atlases "yield the worst measurements and are surpassed by Geometric and Random"** parcellations (k-means on coordinates and Poisson-disk sampling) at the same parcel count. On gender classification "there is no single winner," and the authors' practical advice was that parcellation choice "seems to have a very limited impact" on network analysis ([Arslan et al. 2018](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~sa1013/pub/2017-neuroimage-brain-parcellation-survey.pdf)). Dadi et al. ranked the factors explicitly: the biggest gains come from dimensionality, and the second most important property is softness ([DiFuMo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05405)). Gordon et al. introduced the rotated-parcel null, which keeps parcel shapes and moves them on the sphere ([Gordon 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4677978)). Another evaluation found common anatomical parcellations no better than chance against random partitions ([PMC6519695](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6519695)). Both are snippet-level.

In iEEG the only matched comparison is the data-driven arm. Javadzadeh et al. learned 32-d contrastive coordinates for each channel from LFP in 20 subjects. On a held-out-region test, pooled transformers conditioned on those learned coordinates beat otherwise identical models conditioned on region labels or MNI locations ([NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157293)). This is abstract-level, and it must be cited as prior work for the data-driven-parcellation arm. The EEG controls that exist are test-time interventions, not train-time controls. Permuting positional codes at test time drops REVE from 0.455 to 0.340 balanced accuracy, while BIOT stays near chance either way ([EEG probing paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29367)). CoCoT-EEG reports that shuffling electrode positions "does not consistently degrade performance" ([CoCoT-EEG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09543)). A train-time shuffled-label control at matched K, which keeps the partition but breaks cross-subject correspondence, remains new to iEEG. It is the only arm that separates "anatomy helps" from "any shared categorical grouping helps". Given Arslan's results, the prior hypothesis must not be "atlas beats cubes". The defensible hypothesis is that anatomical labels win only when cross-subject correspondence carries functional meaning that a geometric partition of the same coordinates cannot recover.

### Proposal 3: the probing playbook exists, the iEEG application does not

Vision and language show that learned position IDs recover topology spontaneously. ViT's position embeddings start with no 2-D information and end with row and column structure. Adding 2-D geometry gave no gain over learned 1-D IDs (5-shot 0.6400 vs 0.6421), while dropping position entirely cost about 3 points ([Dosovitskiy et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2010.11929)). Wang & Chen supply the probe battery: linear regression onto absolute position (GPT-2 MAE 1.03 vs BERT 34.14), logistic probes for pairwise order, cosine heatmaps and SVD spectra ([Wang & Chen 2020](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2010.04903)). In neural data, POYO+, NEDS and NuCLR report that region or cell type can be decoded from learned representations, all snippet-level ([POYO+](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10652733); [NEDS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08201.pdf); [NuCLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.01199v1)). PHASE is the nearest iEEG case, but it uses no channel IDs. It probes activity-derived features (lobe macro-F1 0.48 vs BrainBERT 0.41) and matches regions across institutions in 14 of 16 cases ([PHASE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36087v2)). The EEG probing paper measures distance-dependent influence by perturbation (REVE ρ ≈ −0.83, LaBraM ρ ≈ −0.81) rather than regressing embedding geometry on distance ([EEG probing paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.29367)). MAPA never analyzes its region table. One timing result changes the protocol: in matrix-completion transformers, positional-embedding clusters appear **only after the sudden loss drop**, so geometry must be probed at several checkpoints, not only at convergence ([Gopalani et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22244)).

### Proposal 4: target-dependent optima are known in fMRI, untested in iEEG

Dadi et al. found that optimal connectome dimensionality differs by target consistently across atlases: about 50 parcels for Alzheimer's and drug use, about 150 for age, and about 300 for IQ, ASD and PTSD. Task decoding instead improves monotonically up to about 1000 ([DiFuMo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05405)). In iEEG the direct evidence is thin. SEEGformer's coordinate encoding helped epileptogenic-zone localization but not seizure detection ([SEEGformer](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/ebiom/article/PIIS2352-3964(25)00535-3/fulltext)). An EEG benchmark of five positional encodings found that "no single strategy consistently outperforms across tasks" ([Yuce & Stober](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29754)). BaRISTA tested only two language/auditory tasks in its main text. Physiology predicts a locality effect. High-gamma activity in speech cortex is largely decorrelated at 4 mm ([Muller et al. 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5081035)), and high-density µECoG improved speech decoding by 35% ([Duraivel et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-42555-1.pdf)). The counterweight is the fMRI smoothing literature: orientation decoding survived 8 mm smoothing, so robustness to coarse encodings does not prove the signal is non-local ([Hendriks et al. 2017 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurology/articles/10.3389/fneur.2017.00222/full)). MAPA's per-task numbers show visual tasks near chance (frame brightness 0.495, face number 0.515), so those tasks cannot anchor a locality contrast on Brain Treebank ([MAPA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13507)).

### Proposal 5: the ML evidence points to optimization, but neural data has no test

Three verified ML results make the optimization hypothesis credible. Transformer plateaus coincide with **representation collapse, where hidden states across tokens become nearly parallel**, and slow learning of attention maps. Intervening on attention changes plateau length, and the same signatures appear early in Pythia and OLMo pretraining ([Gopalani & Hu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688)). When each component is trained in isolation, **positional embeddings show the longest plateau**, while token embeddings and MLPs show none ([Gopalani et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.22244)). Deep linear networks have symmetry-induced plateaus whose length depends on initialization ([Saxe et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6120)). The closest "spatial info early only" precedent is DroPE: explicit PEs "significantly facilitate pretraining convergence", and they can be dropped after training with under 1% recalibration compute ([DroPE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12167)). Learning using privileged information supplies the formal frame for training-time-only side information ([Lopez-Paz et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03643)). In neural data, the nearest result is MAPA's: no-spatial MAE pretraining underperforms baseline, and models with and without spatial encodings diverge in deeper layers on held-out subjects. That is a representation-depth result, and MAPA reports no loss curves ([MAPA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13507)). The link to the project's own ln 2 plateau is a synthesis, not a published finding. A bidirectional set encoder without channel identity is permutation-equivariant over electrodes, it has no causal mask to leak position, and spatial or ID tokens break that symmetry at initialization.

### Soft assignment: DiFuMo is the only direct soft-vs-hard evidence

Dadi et al. found that "for a given dimensionality, soft modes tend to outperform hard parcellations," and ranked softness second only to dimensionality ([DiFuMo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.05405)). In iEEG, probabilistic labeling exists only as a localization output. Qin et al. give per-contact atlas probabilities ([Qin 2017](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fninf.2017.00010)), iELVis computes proximal tissue density ([iELVis](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/biorxiv/early/2016/11/30/069179.full.pdf)), and ELAS assigns 72.5% of cortical contacts probabilistically ([ELAS](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31437467/)). None of these feeds a decoder. The case for soft labels is label ambiguity. Two semi-automated labeling methods agreed on **only 82% of more than 17,000 contacts** ([Brainstorm sEEG](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12458114/)), and brain shift can reach **10 to 20 mm** for subdural electrodes, less for depth electrodes ([PMC10197594](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10197594/)). Both BaRISTA and MAPA use hard labels. A multi-atlas soft-ROI method exists for fMRI decoding ([Soft-ROI fusion](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.20249v1)). The ambiguity problem grows at Schaefer-400, where parcels shrink toward the scale of localization error. That is exactly where the project's soft-vs-hard test should concentrate.

## Design implications the evidence forces

The first implication is that **parcel count must be matched, or explicitly swept, in every family comparison**. The fMRI literature agrees that granularity dominates family. Without matched K (and ideally matched parcel-size distributions, given Arslan's warning that Dice-type metrics favor uniform parcels), an atlas-vs-cube difference only measures granularity ([Arslan et al. 2018](https://www.doc.ic.ac.uk/~sa1013/pub/2017-neuroimage-brain-parcellation-survey.pdf)). The resolution × data experiment should report its x-axis as **electrodes per region and subjects per region, not only as percent of pretraining data**. BaRISTA's 3.5 to 4.4 electrodes per parcel puts Brain Treebank deep in the variance-limited regime the theory describes, and a subject-count sweep gives the clean n axis that BaRISTA and PopT lacked. Because neither BaRISTA nor MAPA reports seed variance, **multiple seeds per cell** are a cheap, decisive improvement. The partial-pooling literature suggests one more arm: hierarchical embeddings (lobe + parcel + coordinate, with dropout or shrinkage toward the parent) should flatten the optimum curve, and that predicts their behavior across data sizes as well ([Pargent et al. 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00180-022-01207-6)). The continuous counterpart is to sweep the maximum frequency of Fourier coordinate features, which Tancik et al. showed moves from underfitting to overfitting, against N ([Tancik et al.](https://bmild.github.io/fourfeat)).

For the ID-embedding probes, **the shaft confound must be controlled explicitly**. Contacts on one sEEG shaft are both physically adjacent and highly correlated in signal, so an ID embedding can recover "same shaft" from correlation alone. The probes should partial out the same-shaft indicator, test distance only between shafts, and benchmark against a raw signal-correlation baseline. Because learned IDs are per-subject, geometry can be tested only within subject (Mantel/Spearman against MNI distance, ridge regression to x/y/z, logistic probes for hemisphere, lobe and region). Cross-subject anatomy needs PHASE-style region-averaged matching ([PHASE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.36087v2)).

For the optimization question, the literature supplies a concrete arm list. Train with spatial info and then drop it (DroPE-style). Inject spatial info only after N steps into a plateaued run. Use **random fixed per-electrode tags with no geometry**, which is the cleanest contrast between "optimization aid" and "anatomical prior". Train the spatial embeddings in isolation with other components frozen (Gopalani-style). Use shuffled-but-consistent coordinates. The diagnostics come from Gopalani & Hu: mean pairwise cosine similarity of hidden states, attention entropy and output repetition, tracked through the plateau ([Gopalani & Hu 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13688)). For soft assignment, a Gaussian width of **about 2 to 5 mm** matches depth-contact localization error. The 82% inter-method agreement caps the label-noise benefit at about 18% of contacts, and Dadi's ranking predicts that the soft-vs-hard effect will be smaller than a change in K. Power the experiment accordingly. For task locality, use Brain Treebank's auditory and onset tasks (STG-focal) against distributed language features, and avoid the near-chance visual tasks.

## Conclusion

Taken together, the literature casts BaRISTA's "parcels beat lobes and channels" as one point on a curve that theory says should move. With about four electrodes per parcel, Destrieux sits near the variance-limited optimum for 10 subjects, not at a universal optimum. That makes the project's main claim falsifiable in a sharp way. If the optimal K does not grow with the number of subjects, or if matched-K random cubes do as well as atlases, then the field's preference for anatomical tables reflects data density, not anatomy. Either outcome is publishable, and in both cases the BaRISTA and MAPA results fall out as special cases, which is the right relationship to claim with them.

The second takeaway concerns which question to lead with. MAPA's most striking result, pretraining without spatial codes falling *below* baseline, fits an optimization account better than a representational one, and the ML plateau literature supplies the mechanism. The random-tag-vs-anatomy contrast is therefore the single experiment most likely to change how the field reads every prior spatial-encoding ablation. If random tags rescue training as well as anatomy does, much of the reported "spatial benefit" in iEEG foundation models is symmetry breaking, and the resolution question applies mainly to what remains after the plateau is escaped.
