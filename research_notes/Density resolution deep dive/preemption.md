# Pre-emption search: resolution of electrode-location encoding x data density
Date: 2026-11-20. Claim tested: the optimal spatial resolution of the location encoding (coarse to fine grids or atlases) shifts finer as the number of training subjects/electrodes per region grows (bias-variance / partitioning estimator), tested cross-subject.

## Method
- Semantic Scholar citations API (fetched 2026-11-20): BaRISTA arXiv:2512.12135 (10 citing papers), PopT arXiv:2406.03044 (33), Neuroprobe arXiv:2509.21671 (5), MAPA arXiv:2609.13507 (0, not yet indexed as cited). All titles and abstracts screened.
- OpenReview API full-text search (titles/abstracts), which includes ICLR 2027 submissions under review.
- arXiv API keyword queries (spatial encoding, parcellation, region embedding, positional encoding, number of training subjects, bias-variance + atlas). Only previously known papers came back.
- Web searches (iEEG/ECoG/EEG/MEG/spiking/fMRI).
- Google Scholar "cited by" was NOT checked (no access). That gap remains.

Legend: [FULL] = PDF text extracted and the relevant sections read or grepped; [ABS] = abstract or listing page only.

## Ranked overlap (most to least threatening)
1. **BaRISTA** (arXiv 2512.12135, NeurIPS 2025) [FULL]. Compares channel (LPI coordinates), Destrieux parcel and lobe scales for both encoding and masking. Parcel encoding wins. Data sweep: 5/10/25/50/75% of pretraining data, **only for the parcels/channels config**, plus one held-out-subject test. It never crosses scale with data amount. This is still the closest prior work and the main reviewer comparison. Its Discussion lists learned or other spatial scales as future work.
2. **Javadzadeh et al., "Learning Data-Driven Shared Coordinates for Cross-Subject Intracranial Recordings"**, NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Symmetry and Geometry in Neural Representations. https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157293 ; OpenReview gCNfBBTNWF [ABS]. 20 subjects, basal ganglia and thalamus LFP (DBS cohort, not Brain Treebank). A contrastive CNN learns 32-d coordinates, with 52.84% held-out-channel k-NN accuracy. In held-out-region waveform prediction, pooled transformers conditioned on learned coordinates beat region labels and MNI. **No sweep of training subjects and no sweep of resolution.**
   - Related versions: arXiv 2510.27090 (ICLR 2026 submission) [FULL]. It compares only MNI vs two functional embeddings on 11 subjects, with no subject sweep. The ICLR 2027 submission "FunctionalMap" (OpenReview mvhZgiG0fa) [ABS] reports that "Performance improves with more training subjects". From the abstract this looks like a sweep for the functional method only, not crossed with encoding type or resolution. The PDF was not retrievable (403), so **verify this**.
3. **UniPopT** (ICLR 2027 submission, OpenReview JcIG5NkCvk) [ABS]. Analyses how decoding scales with fine-tuning data per subject and finds "most of the gain from additional electrodes arrives through the spherical coordinates". This links the presence of a coordinate encoding to data amount, but the resolution is not varied. Read it once the PDF is accessible.
4. **Device Passport** (arXiv 2607.00249) [FULL, grepped]. Scalp EEG (TUH). Compares channel-embedding types (APE, channel ID, XYZ, ACPE, its own method) as a function of the number of **fine-tuning** subjects under layout mismatch. This crosses encoding *type* with data amount, but not resolution, and it is not iEEG.
5. **DIVER-1** (arXiv 2512.19097) [FULL, grepped]. Subject-diversity sweep (2 to 16 subjects at fixed volume), with a non-monotonic result. Ablation: the absolute 3D MNI position embedding hurts on Neuroprobe but helps when pretraining is matched on BTB. Not crossed with resolution.
6. **MAPA** (arXiv 2609.13507) [FULL, grepped]. One DKT+aseg table (74 labels) plus relative PE, with an ablation that removes either encoding. 50 of the 74 regions appear in pretraining and 4 rows are never trained, which is relevant to the sparsity argument. No sweep of granularity or subjects.
7. **iMINDBench** (arXiv 2609.18104) [FULL, grepped]. Scaling study: up to 25x more supervised data from other subjects or institutions gives small or task-dependent gains. Its BaRISTA baseline is run at Destrieux-ASEG only. No resolution sweep.
8. Low overlap [ABS]: PHASE (coordinate-free), DANCE (ICLR 2027 submission; coordinate-free, connectivity-based), NeuroCLUS (ICML 2026; learned functional clusters), RECTOR (adaptive functional partitions), Neuro-MoBRE (region experts), WiCAT (arXiv 2607.09754; atlas tokenization for widefield calcium imaging), Seizure Embedding Map (JNE 2026; DKT positional encoding), CORTEG, iBrain, PUPPET (ICLR 2027 submission), BrainWideBench, POYO+/NEDS (unit embeddings; no grid of region granularity vs data found).
9. Adjacent precedent outside iEEG: fMRI connectome prediction studies compare parcel counts (100/400/1000; "finer up to ~400 ROIs") and separately study sample size. Web search found no study crossing the two. Cite these as conceptual precedent and run a dedicated search before writing related work.

## Residual risk
- No paper found crosses resolution of the location encoding with the number of training subjects, in any modality.
- Gaps: Google Scholar cited-by was not checked. MAPA has no indexed citers yet. ICLR 2027 submissions (FunctionalMap, UniPopT) were seen as abstracts only. NeurIPS 2026 workshop papers are poorly indexed, so only the one workshop paper above was found.
- Most likely scoops: (a) a BaRISTA follow-up from the Shanechi lab crossing channel/parcel/lobe with the data fraction, which is the natural extension of their Fig. 5; (b) FunctionalMap's subject sweep, if it turns out to include the region-label and MNI baselines; (c) fMRI parcellation-by-sample-size work, which would weaken the "theory is new" framing more than the empirical novelty.
- Recommendation: set alerts for BaRISTA and MAPA citations. Re-check the OpenReview PDFs for FunctionalMap and UniPopT when they become accessible.
